MLB ballparks from Fenway to Dodger Stadium

All 30 major league ballparks as of 2026, from Fenway and Wrigley to the Athletics' temporary home in West Sacramento, with a classic local place to eat or drink near about half of them.

Northeast

Attraction · Fenway-Kenmore

Fenway Park

The oldest park in the majors, open since 1912. Look for the single red seat high in the right-field bleachers that marks Ted Williams's longest home run, and Pesky's Pole down the short right-field line.

4 Jersey St, Boston, MA 02215

Bar · Fenway-Kenmore

Cask'n Flagon

The sports bar across Brookline Avenue from the back of the Green Monster, pouring since 1969. Gets loud before first pitch and stays that way after the last out.

62 Brookline Ave, Boston, MA 02215

Attraction · The Bronx

Yankee Stadium

Get in early for Monument Park behind the center-field fence, with plaques and retired numbers going back to Babe Ruth. It closes before first pitch; check the team site for the cutoff.

1 E 161st St, Bronx, NY 10451

Bar · The Bronx

Yankee Tavern

An old-school tavern a block from the stadium, around since the 1920s, with Yankees history on every wall and a pub menu that holds up before or after a game.

72 E 161st St, Bronx, NY 10451

Attraction · Flushing

Citi Field

Enter through the Jackie Robinson Rotunda, modeled on Ebbets Field. The old Home Run Apple from Shea Stadium sits outside the gate, and a newer one rises in center field after Mets homers.

41 Seaver Way, Queens, NY 11368

Attraction · South Philadelphia

Citizens Bank Park

Walk Ashburn Alley behind the outfield for cheesesteaks and the Phillies' Wall of Fame. The giant neon Liberty Bell in right-center rings and lights up after Phillies home runs.

1 Citizens Bank Way, Philadelphia, PA 19148

Attraction · North Shore

PNC Park

Arguably the best view in baseball: downtown Pittsburgh framed over the outfield across the Allegheny. The classic approach is on foot from downtown over the yellow Roberto Clemente Bridge.

115 Federal St, Pittsburgh, PA 15212

Restaurant · Strip District

Primanti Bros.

The original Strip District counter, where the fries and coleslaw go inside the sandwich. About two miles up the river from PNC Park; expect a line on game days.

46 18th St, Pittsburgh, PA 15222

Attraction · Downtown Baltimore

Oriole Park at Camden Yards

The 1992 park that set the template for the retro ballparks that followed. Eutaw Street runs between the stands and the long brick B&O Warehouse, with brass plaques where home runs landed and Boog's BBQ smoking away.

333 W Camden St, Baltimore, MD 21201

Bar · Sowebo

Pickles Pub

A sports bar across Washington Boulevard from the ballpark, with a back patio and a crowd in orange before every home game. The default pregame stop for Orioles fans.

520 Washington Blvd, Baltimore, MD 21230

Attraction · Navy Yard

Nationals Park

Stay in your seat for the fourth-inning Presidents Race, with giant-headed Washington, Jefferson, Lincoln and Teddy Roosevelt sprinting down the warning track. The Anacostia riverfront is a short walk away.

1500 S Capitol St SE, Washington, DC 20003

Restaurant · U Street

Ben's Chili Bowl

A U Street landmark since 1958 that kept serving through decades of change in the neighborhood. Order the chili half-smoke. It's about three miles north of the ballpark, a straight shot on the Green Line.

1213 U St NW, Washington, DC 20009

Attraction · Downtown Toronto

Rogers Centre

The first ballpark with a fully retractable roof, under the CN Tower. A hotel has rooms that look straight onto the field, and the renovated outfield now has open-air social decks. Bring a passport.

1 Blue Jays Way, Toronto, ON M5V 1J1, Canada

Southeast

Attraction · The Battery Atlanta

Truist Park

Out in Cobb County, about ten miles northwest of downtown, inside the Battery's restaurants and bars. The street number is a nod to Hank Aaron's 755 home runs; his statue stands in the Monument Garden on the concourse.

755 Battery Ave SE, Atlanta, GA 30339

Attraction · Little Havana

loanDepot park

A retractable-roof park on the old Orange Bowl site. The colorful home run sculpture that once sat in center field now stands out on the plaza, which is the best place for photos.

501 Marlins Way, Miami, FL 33125

Restaurant · Little Havana

Versailles Restaurant

Miami's best-known Cuban restaurant, on Calle Ocho since 1971. Order a cafecito and a pastry at the walk-up window if you don't want a full meal of ropa vieja. About two miles from the ballpark.

3555 SW 8th St, Miami, FL 33135

Attraction · St. Petersburg

Tropicana Field

The Rays came home in 2026 after Hurricane Milton tore off the roof in 2024. It's the only fixed dome in the majors, with catwalks that have their own ground rules. Check team news on plans for a new ballpark.

1 Tropicana Dr, St. Petersburg, FL 33705

Midwest

Attraction · Wrigleyville

Wrigley Field

Open since 1914, with ivy on the outfield brick and a center-field scoreboard still changed by hand. Look across Waveland and Sheffield for the rooftop bleachers on the apartment buildings.

1060 W Addison St, Chicago, IL 60613

Bar · Wrigleyville

Murphy's Bleachers

The corner bar at Sheffield and Waveland, steps from the bleacher gate. A Wrigleyville fixture that fills up hours before first pitch and empties straight into the bleachers.

3655 N Sheffield Ave, Chicago, IL 60613

Attraction · Bridgeport

Rate Field

The White Sox park on the South Side, with an exploding scoreboard whose pinwheels spin after home runs, carried over from old Comiskey Park. Comiskey's home plate is marked in the parking lot to the north.

333 W 35th St, Chicago, IL 60616

Attraction · Milwaukee

American Family Field

Arrive early: tailgating in the parking lots is part of the ritual here. Inside, watch for Bernie Brewer's slide after Brewers home runs and the sausage race in the sixth inning under the fan-shaped retractable roof.

1 Brewers Way, Milwaukee, WI 53214

Attraction · North Loop

Target Field

Built with Minnesota limestone and squeezed into the North Loop, with light rail stopping at the gate. The Minnie and Paul sign in center field lights up when the Twins homer.

1 Twins Way, Minneapolis, MN 55403

Attraction · Downtown Detroit

Comerica Park

Big stone tigers guard the gates, and inside there's a carousel and a Ferris wheel with baseball-shaped cars. After the Fifth Third and Comerica bank merger, expect a new name; check the Tigers site for the current one.

2100 Woodward Ave, Detroit, MI 48201

Restaurant · Downtown Detroit

American Coney Island

The Detroit coney dog, with chili, mustard and onions, served since 1917 right next door to its rival, Lafayette Coney Island. About a mile south of the ballpark.

114 W Lafayette Blvd, Detroit, MI 48226

Attraction · Gateway District

Progressive Field

Opened as Jacobs Field in 1994 and recently renovated. Heritage Park, behind the center-field wall, honors the Guardians' greats, and the downtown skyline rises over left field.

2401 Ontario St, Cleveland, OH 44115

Bar · Ohio City

Great Lakes Brewing Co.

A brewpub since 1988 in old brick buildings next to the West Side Market, across the river from the ballpark. Order the Edmund Fitzgerald porter.

2516 Market Ave, Cleveland, OH 44113

Attraction · The Banks

Great American Ball Park

Twin riverboat smokestacks beyond right-center shoot fireworks after Reds home runs. The Reds Hall of Fame and Museum is next to the main gate, and the Ohio River runs past the outfield.

100 Joe Nuxhall Way, Cincinnati, OH 45202

Bar · Downtown Cincinnati

Arnold's Bar and Grill

Cincinnati's oldest tavern, open since 1861, with a covered courtyard out back. About a mile north of the ballpark.

210 E 8th St, Cincinnati, OH 45202

Attraction · Downtown St. Louis

Busch Stadium

Upper-deck seats behind home plate frame the Gateway Arch over the outfield. Ballpark Village across Clark Avenue holds the Cardinals Hall of Fame and Museum.

700 Clark Ave, St. Louis, MO 63102

Attraction · Truman Sports Complex

Kauffman Stadium

The fountains and waterfalls beyond the outfield wall are the largest privately funded fountain display in the world. Grab a seat out in the outfield to get misted on a hot night.

1 Royal Way, Kansas City, MO 64129

Southwest

Attraction · Arlington

Globe Life Field

An air-conditioned park with a retractable roof, built so Texas summers don't wreck the game. It sits between the Rangers' old park and the Cowboys' stadium, with the Texas Live! district across the street.

734 Stadium Dr, Arlington, TX 76011

Attraction · Downtown Houston

Daikin Park

The Astros' park, called Minute Maid Park until 2025, built into the old Union Station. A replica locomotive rolls along the left-field wall after Astros home runs, above the short porch of the Crawford Boxes.

501 Crawford St, Houston, TX 77002

Restaurant · Second Ward

The Original Ninfa's on Navigation

The East End Tex-Mex restaurant Mama Ninfa Laurenzo opened in 1973, credited with putting fajitas on the map in Houston. About a mile east of the ballpark.

2704 Navigation Blvd, Houston, TX 77003

Attraction · Downtown Phoenix

Chase Field

There's a swimming pool beyond the right-center field wall, rented as a suite. The infield keeps an old-fashioned dirt path from the mound to home plate, and the roof closes for summer heat.

401 E Jefferson St, Phoenix, AZ 85004

Attraction · Ballpark

Coors Field

A row of purple seats in the upper deck marks exactly one mile above sea level. Balls fly in the thin air, which is why the Rockies keep game balls in a humidor.

2001 Blake St, Denver, CO 80205

Bar · LoDo

Wynkoop Brewing Company

Colorado's first brewpub, opened in 1988 in an old LoDo warehouse two blocks from Coors Field. Pool tables upstairs and plenty of room for a postgame crowd.

1634 18th St, Denver, CO 80202

West

Attraction · Elysian Park

Dodger Stadium

Open since 1962 in Chavez Ravine, with palm trees and the San Gabriel Mountains beyond the outfield at sunset. Have a Dodger Dog, and stay for the view from the top deck as the sun goes down.

1000 Vin Scully Ave, Los Angeles, CA 90012

Restaurant · Chinatown

Philippe the Original

A 1908 deli near Union Station that claims to have invented the French dip. Order it double-dipped with a dab of the hot mustard. About a mile and a half from Dodger Stadium.

1001 N Alameda St, Los Angeles, CA 90012

Attraction · Anaheim

Angel Stadium

The giant Big A sign in the parking lot lights its halo after Angels wins. Beyond center field, a fake rock formation with waterfalls and geysers goes off after home runs.

2000 E Gene Autry Way, Anaheim, CA 92806

Attraction · East Village

Petco Park

The old Western Metal Supply Co. building is built into the left-field corner, with seats on its roof. Gallagher Square, the grassy area beyond the outfield, is a low-cost way to watch a game from the lawn.

100 Park Blvd, San Diego, CA 92101

Attraction · South Beach

Oracle Park

Home runs over the right-field wall land in McCovey Cove, where kayakers wait for splash hits. Get the garlic fries and bring a jacket; night games turn cold off the bay.

24 Willie Mays Plaza, San Francisco, CA 94107

Attraction · West Sacramento

Sutter Health Park

The Athletics' temporary home through 2027, before their planned move to Las Vegas, shared with the Triple-A River Cats. The smallest park in the majors, with a lawn berm in the outfield and the gold Tower Bridge a short walk away.

400 Ballpark Dr, West Sacramento, CA 95691

Attraction · SoDo

T-Mobile Park

The retractable roof works like an umbrella: it covers the field when it rains but leaves the sides open. The upper concourse looks out over downtown and Elliott Bay.

1250 1st Ave S, Seattle, WA 98134
